# Taglish notes (checkout and cashier copy)

Research was thin. Web search found little on PH food-shop POS copy, PUP Sta. Mesa stalls, or "solb". Anything not tied to a URL below is marked **suggestion**.

## What sources support
- Taglish is common in Philippine media and digital content and reads as relatable: https://www.globalizationpartners.com/2024/11/27/taglish-linguistic-blend-filipino-identity/
- "po" signals politeness toward older people, bosses, and customers: https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/news/po-in-tagalog-meaning-and (a usage guide, not UX research)
- "Sulit" (worth it) is used by eateries near campuses for combo sets and student deals: https://3d-universal.com/en/blogs/where-to-eat-in-baguio-on-a-student-budget.html
- "Pasok sa budget" appears in a GMA Pop Talk review of a student-friendly eatery: https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/story/319265
- Portion size pitched as value for students (no "busog" used): https://varsitarian.net/sports/sports/20080202/hop_in_the_thomasian_pocket_friendly_food_trip
- Student meal budgets of about P100-200 (Baguio, not Manila): same 3d-universal link.
- Not found: any source for "solb" or for PUP-specific wording. Treat "solb" as unverified slang.

## Tone guide (all suggestion)
- Warm and short, like a cashier talking: Taglish, not deep Tagalog.
- Use "po" on customer-facing thank-yous and prompts. Skip it on cashier-only buttons to save space.
- Keep money, product names, and button verbs in English (Checkout, Undo, Remove). Cashiers scan these fast.
- Max 3 words on buttons.

## Do / Don't (suggestion)
- Do: "Salamat po!", "Sulit!", "Bayad na" for confirmations.
- Do: put Taglish flavor in empty states and success messages, where nothing is at risk.
- Don't: slang on money amounts, errors, or destructive actions. Keep them plain.
- Don't: "solb", "lodi", "charot" in UI. Unsourced, can read as trying too hard.
- Don't: mix three languages or add emoji walls.
